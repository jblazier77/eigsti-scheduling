## Dates without mandatory times

Josh clarified that leads can place one or several jobs on the same day without selecting time slots. The default is No time set (`day`), with start/end dates and no invented start/end hours. Shared-day assignments show a workload warning but can coexist. This applies to every named participant: untimed plans are commitments to review, not exclusive hourly reservations. Exact daily ordering is not automatically inferred from visit creation or queue priority.

Morning, afternoon, Reserve all day and specific hours are optional exclusive reservations. Both tentative and confirmed explicit blocks reserve named people; waiting/readiness is independent and is not confirmed by scheduling. Mixed explicit blocks conflict on [start,end), allowing adjacent intervals. Explicit reservations ignore untimed plans for hard conflicts but display the shared-day warning. A date-only plan does not claim that someone is free for a full additional day's work.

Dates stay PostgreSQL date. Display/calculation never interprets a date string as the user's local midnight. Company calendar: America/Chicago, weekdays 07:00–15:30, morning ends at noon, afternoon starts at noon. Multi-day visits include both endpoints and skip weekends. The current slice caps a visit at 31 calendar days; timed visits must be one weekday and within company hours. Weekend/overtime/holiday exceptions remain undefined and unimplemented. A multi-day crew must stay the same; split visits when different people attend different days.

## Workflow and scope

Job details gains Scheduled visits with an inline Plan a visit form. PM chooses a work portion and active coordinating lead. A lead creates a visit for work they own, coordinating it themselves. On a saved visit, PM or its current coordinating lead expands Edit visit & staffing directly inside the job or schedule card. Dates/help needed and named help remain separate narrow saves on that same screen. Cancel resets only its draft without reloading the page. Existing /visits/[id] links redirect to the job card. Staffing choices load on expansion rather than on every calendar card. This is the default approach for remaining development under D40. New/edited visits do not change work ownership or queue order. A coordinating lead may continue to coordinate an existing visit after work ownership changes; PM must explicitly change that visit's coordinator if needed.

Named help is saved separately from dates/required help. An empty helper need is visible. Active helpers or leads may help; Helping is a visit duty and never changes account roles. PM can explicitly replace a roster. The coordinating lead can only preserve existing names and fill vacant places; neither reducing required help nor replacing names can silently remove staff. Busy checks return person identity plus a busy flag, never another customer's title/address. People on untimed plans are not marked as exclusively busy. No drag, notifications or automatic displacement/rescheduling.

Schedule footer link opens a day list by default with a Week calendar toggle. Week is five dated columns (stacked on narrow screens). PM defaults My jobs and can switch to All jobs. Leads/helpers see their own named visits, including Leading or Helping; relevant job details remain separately scoped. A helper does not gain the company schedule. The calendar is a dated card view, not a vertical hourly grid.

Cancel scheduled visit is explicit and releases reservations for the entire crew, retaining the visit/participants and audit history. This slice does not implement visit completion, portion completion, return-work commands, job closing or availability exceptions. Existing planned reservations are not automatically cleared by future direct administrative job/work lifecycle changes; those future supported commands must resolve visits explicitly and preserve the lock protocol. Do not use raw SQL lifecycle edits as an operational scheduling workflow.

## Atomicity and access

0008 introduces visits, visit_participants, private visit_bookings and private visit_events. Raw employee table access is denied; scoped security-definer RPCs enforce live roles, work/visit relationships, current coordinator, eligible active staff, capacity and exact overlaps. No public signup or administrative key. Date-only shared-day warnings contain a count only, not unrelated customer details.

All schedule writes acquire a company-wide scheduling advisory transaction lock before parent job/work/visit locks. This intentionally serializes schedule mutations for the initial small-company workload. Actor profile and assigned profiles are share-locked, calendar locks are acquired in sorted UUID order, then all old/new reservations and named job entitlements change in the same transaction. Save never locks a queue; no reverse queue->job dependency is added. Future deactivation/close/multi-job commands must follow this protocol. These invariants have not yet been proven with real concurrent database connections.

Timed conflicts abort the whole command, preserving visit, roster, bookings, entitlements and history. No conflicting visit is moved. Narrow date/plan patches do not replace participants. Actor/operation/request receipts serialize retries; changed payloads reject, identical old retries return current authorized results without replaying old dates/staffing. An uncertain response retains the same request and freezes its draft for retry. Fresh confirmed commands rotate request IDs. Audit snapshots retain visit plus full roster; raw history is private and no history UI is added.

Named participants receive helping_assignment job entitlements. Removing a name or cancelling the final noncancelled visit revokes that source of access, preserving independent work ownership or another visit. Noncancelled completed visits will preserve job information until job closure, as proposed in permissions.md; completion commands remain a later slice. Cancelled participants may still retrieve their own visit summary through the scoped visit endpoint, but lose job/file access when no other entitlement exists.
